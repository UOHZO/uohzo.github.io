Abstract

Interaction-rule construction for attack graphs is decomposed into four stages, with multi-task fine-tuning, curriculum learning, and Focal Loss improving the ability to learn complex rules.

Full research process

This paper is still under submission/review, so the full research process, experimental results, and implementation details are not disclosed at this stage. The following introduces only the research problem, methodological approach, and my contributions.

The research question is: how can a natural-language vulnerability description be automatically converted into interaction rules that a logical attack graph can use directly to infer multistage attack paths?

Existing methods suffer from limited predicate coverage, high expert construction costs, and inaccurate automatic construction. The challenges are primarily technical: vulnerability descriptions may omit product functions and operating mechanisms; predicates, parameters, and causal relationships within rules must remain consistent; and one-shot end-to-end generation places too many reasoning demands on a single step.

INTERNIST is an automated interaction rule construction framework. It builds a general predicate taxonomy from cybersecurity states and converts vulnerability descriptions into interaction rules through four stages: predicate extraction → parameter association → parameter filling → causal organization.

  • General predicate modeling: Define 19 categories of general predicates based on cybersecurity states in SPARTA, supporting 20+ attack types and broadening attack graph coverage across scenarios.
  • Staged rule construction: Decompose complex end-to-end generation into four interdependent subtasks, reducing the rule reasoning difficulty at each stage.
  • Collaborative multitask learning: Combine LoRA multitask fine-tuning, curriculum learning, and Focal Loss to share semantic knowledge across stages and focus on complex and insufficiently learned samples.
  • Knowledge-enhanced construction: Supplement external knowledge about product functions and operating mechanisms, together with few-shot prompting, to address missing information and semantic ambiguity.

These quantities and the methodological scope come from the research summary; specific experimental settings and results are not disclosed at this stage.

I participated in key method design and technical discussions, helped refine the research narrative and articulation of the core contributions, and was responsible for designing and drawing figures throughout the paper to improve its logical flow and presentation.
