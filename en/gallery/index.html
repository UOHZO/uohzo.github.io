Reflections on SIGIR 2026

In July 2026, I attended SIGIR 2026 in Melbourne, Australia, and gave an oral presentation of my first-author paper, How Far Are We from Automatically Identifying Violations of the Data Minimization Principle in Privacy Policies?, at the Melbourne Convention and Exhibition Centre. It was my first time attending a conference abroad, and I learned a great deal!

Read the full story

SIGIR focuses primarily on search, advertising, and recommendation, while my paper fell within a privacy-related subtopic. Attending the conference helped me see the many connections between privacy and security and these areas. In conversations with researchers from different universities and people working in industry, privacy was a shared concern, and we often discussed the importance of retrieval for improving large language models. These exchanges reinforced my sense that, although my background is in security, I need to keep learning about developments in large language models and retrieval and find meaningful ways to connect them with security problems.

Another lasting impression was that communication and expression are part of being a researcher. Many researchers were skilled at drawing out the ideas behind a paper and presenting them as simple, clear methods and ways of thinking. I used to feel, at times, that a more complex method made the work seem more substantial. These conversations strengthened my belief that what matters is thinking through the problem, identifying the key points, and solving it as simply and effectively as possible. This is something I will keep reminding myself of in my future research and work.
